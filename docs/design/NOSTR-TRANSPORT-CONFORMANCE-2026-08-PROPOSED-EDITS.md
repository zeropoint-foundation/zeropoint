## The argument in one paragraph

§3.2's reasoning is content-blind: *"A carrier that forwards and forgets is a medium. A carrier that retains is a custodian, and a custodian is a party."* Read literally it applies to any retained payload. But a custodian is a party because it holds something over which it has **discretion** — to disclose, withhold, alter, or correlate. A chain-head commitment gives it none: it cannot alter one (signed), cannot learn from one (a hash), cannot meaningfully withhold one (multi-relay publication is the native mode, and §2 already notes this *"makes withholding require collusion"*), and disclosure is the entire intent. The relay is not custodying the operator's business; it is echoing a notice posted on purpose.

The discriminating property is not kinship-versus-commitment. It is **whether the payload's value comes from being delivered or from being findable** — and that distinction happens to have a mechanically checkable proxy, which is what makes it implementable rather than merely persuasive.

## E2 — §3.2: restate the ruling on the property that discriminates

Proposed replacement for the ruling sentence and the paragraph following it. The reasoning already in §3.2 is retained verbatim below the new statement; only the scope changes.

> **Ruling: no *addressed* cross-sovereign payload persists on a third party.** A payload is addressed if it names a recipient — in Nostr terms, if it carries a `p` tag. Addressed kinship-scoped traffic uses ephemeral kinds only, 21059 for wrapped payloads per §3.1. **Unaddressed payloads — commitments published to be found rather than delivered to anyone — are not constrained by this ruling**, and are governed instead by the contract that owns their content.
>
> **Why addressing is the right discriminator, and not merely a convenient one.** The medium/custodian distinction turns on discretion, and discretion is what addressing creates. An addressed payload is *someone's business passing through*: retaining it lets the relay disclose, withhold, or correlate a relationship that was never its own. An unaddressed commitment is a public assertion with no counterparty — the relay cannot alter it (signed), cannot read it (a hash), and cannot suppress it (multi-relay). It has nothing to be discreet about. A carrier that retains an addressed payload becomes a custodian; a carrier that retains an unaddressed one is a bulletin board, which is infrastructure the operator used deliberately rather than a party to anything.
>
> Stated as the underlying property: **delivery-valued payloads must be ephemeral, findability-valued payloads must not be.** For a commitment, ephemerality is not a constraint but a contradiction — §3 already establishes that *"a witness you control witnesses nothing,"* and a witness that forgets witnesses nothing either. The rule as originally written would have made the External row of the payload table unimplementable.

**Ruling requested.** The reasoning is drafted; the decision is the operator's.

## E3 — NT9: extend the type-level enforcement to the retention discriminator

NT9 currently requires that the adapter's kinship publish surface accept ephemeral kinds only, *"enforced by type rather than by convention,"* and states that this *"is the property that keeps a relay a medium rather than a party, so it is the one that must not be a lint."*

That requirement is unchanged and should be widened rather than relaxed, because the amendment makes the distinction structural rather than categorical:

> **NT9 (amended)** — No code path publishes an *addressed* payload to a persisted kind. The adapter's publish surface is split by addressing at the type level: a constructor that takes a recipient produces a value that can only be published to an ephemeral kind, and a constructor that takes none produces a value that may be published to either. Attempting to publish an addressed payload to kind 1059 is a compile-time or construction-time failure, not a runtime warning. **The two constructors must be separately named**, because an adapter with one notion of "publish" will get this wrong the same way §6 warns an adapter with one notion of "keypair" will.

This is deliberately the same discipline §6 already applies to identity keys versus NIP-59 envelope keys: the categories must stay nameable in the code, or the distinction survives only as long as whoever wrote it is still reading.

## E4 — §11 Open position C: record why this is not the Tier 2 trigger

C's resolution states that if durable cross-sovereign relay storage is ever proposed, *"the relay becomes a party, C reopens, and it is a Tier 2 contract amendment — a `CROSS-SUBSTRATE-PEER-CONTRACT` §8 autoregressive update trigger — not a design-note decision."*

This amendment must say plainly whether it trips that clause. **The position drafted here is that it does not**, and the reason should be recorded rather than assumed:

> C's condition is scoped to *cross-sovereign payloads* — traffic between sovereigns, which is what `CROSS-SUBSTRATE-PEER-CONTRACT` governs. A chain-head commitment is not addressed to a peer and has no counterparty; it never engages that contract, because there is no exchange for the contract to govern. Its retention is therefore a question for `EXTERNAL-ANCHOR-TIER-CONTRACT`, which has **already ruled on this payload by content** in §5 Forbidden #5 by permitting the compact commitment across the boundary. Extending a transport ruling to override that would leave two contracts governing one object under incompatible logics.
>
> C accordingly remains resolved and its condition remains armed: durable storage of an **addressed** cross-sovereign payload still reopens it as a Tier 2 amendment. The amendment narrows what the condition covers; it does not disarm it.

**Ruling requested.** If the operator reads C's condition as covering any retained payload rather than any retained *addressed* payload, then this edit sheet is a Tier 2 contract amendment and should be re-scoped as one rather than applied as a design-note edit.

## E6 — cross-document: `ANCHOR-BACKEND-SELECTION-2026-08.md`, narrow the published commitment

The enumeration-surface objection survives this amendment and should be answered by narrowing the payload rather than by forbidding retention.

§4 Slot 1 of the conformance document already makes the argument for announces: a persisted record becomes *"a permanently queryable record of substrate existence, cadence, and capability set — an enumeration surface, and one that outlives any revocation."* A persisted commitment is the same shape, and unlike kinship traffic it cannot be gift-wrapped, because findability is the point.

`AnchorCommitment` currently carries `chain_sequence` alongside `chain_head_hash`. On the **external** publication path it should not:

> Hash plus operator signature is sufficient for every property §2 requires — precedence, independence, verifiability, non-suppressibility. `chain_sequence` buys the verifier nothing and hands an observer the substrate's chain growth rate for free. Retain it on the internal path where the fleet is both author and audience; strip it at the external boundary.

Cadence still leaks and that residual is irreducible — publication is the point. Event-driven anchoring blunts it, since audits and disputes are irregular where a timer would be a signature. **This should be stated as a residual rather than papered over**, in the same register as §3.1's accounting of what gift wrap does and does not conceal.

## What this does not resolve

- **Whether relays are worth using for commitments at all.** The amendment makes it permissible, not advisable. Relay-published commitments give a third party existence-and-time without operator cooperation; whether that is worth the enumeration surface is a deployment judgment, and `ANCHOR-BACKEND-SELECTION` §6.1's floor/escalation split does not depend on it.
- **Open position A.** Whether an off-the-shelf relay honours ephemeral-kind non-persistence is unchanged and still measured at m0. The amendment increases what rides on it: NT2 now verifies the ephemeral half of a two-tier rule rather than a blanket one, so a relay that persists ephemeral kinds now breaks a distinction rather than a policy.
- **Whether `chain_sequence` is load-bearing anywhere else.** Checked 2026-08-14: the field is declared at `crates/zp-anchor/src/lib.rs:117` and populated once at `crates/zp-server/src/anchor_pipeline.rs:182`. **No code reads it.** E6 is therefore a producer-side change with no consumer to break, and now is the cheapest moment it will ever be.

## A correction this turned up

`ANCHOR-BACKEND-SELECTION-2026-08.md` §1 says the anchor is *"the component least implemented,"* which overstates it. `crates/zp-server/src/anchor_pipeline.rs` exists and is tested — trigger taxonomy over receipt types and system events, `classify` firing on gate denials, skip logic for epoch-anchor receipts, and an `end_to_end_event_driven_seal` test. The event-driven discipline the trait's design notes call for is built and green.

What is missing is specifically the **outbound backend** — the thing that carries a commitment to a witness. That is a narrower and more actionable gap than "the anchor is unimplemented," and it means an `anchor()` implementation lands into working plumbing rather than into nothing. §1 of that memo should be corrected accordingly.
